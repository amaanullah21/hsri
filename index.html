Hafzan’s Stagflation Resilience Index (HSRI)

The HSRI is a novel composite measure designed to evaluate a nation's capacity to withstand stagflationary pressures by integrating labor market dynamics and monetary flow theory.

HSRI = (Productivity Growth × Money Velocity) / (Unemployment Rate × Inflation Rate)

Understanding the Ranges

How should we interpret the HSRI score? Because this index is a ratio of growth drivers vs. economic stressors, the following benchmarks apply:

HSRI Range Classification Economic Meaning
< 0.10 Critical Active stagflation. The economy has no "buffer" to protect against price and job shocks.
0.10 – 0.50 Vulnerable Normal but fragile. Growth and velocity are barely keeping pace with stressors.
0.50 – 2.00 Resilient Healthy adaptability. Typical of stable, productive economies.
> 2.00 Elite / Outlier Exceptional resilience. Usually found in hyper-productive phases or tech booms.
> 5.00 Overheating? "Too High": While high resilience is good, a score above 5.0 may indicate an outlier. It could mean money velocity is unsustainably fast or inflation is near-zero, which might signal a different risk (deflationary trap).
